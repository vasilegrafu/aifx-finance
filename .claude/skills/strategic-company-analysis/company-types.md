# company-types — emphasis adjustments once the type is known

Load once, after the company is defined. These adjust *emphasis* inside the
modules — they replace nothing. A company can be several types at once
(a mature regulated platform); apply each relevant section.

## Private companies

Financials are partial or absent — say so rather than pretending. Use public
information, industry benchmarks, and cautious inference; label every
estimated figure as an estimate and never let one masquerade as reported.
Weight shifts to what is observable: business model, customer economics,
positioning, capabilities, industry structure. The fact pack covers listed
peers — use it for benchmarks, not for the subject.

## Startups and high-growth companies

Traditional ratios are premature; do not lean on them. What matters:
product-market fit evidence, unit economics (CAC vs LTV, contribution margin
at maturity, not blended today), retention cohorts, monetization path,
scaling economics (do margins improve with scale, or does growth buy revenue
at constant cost?), capital runway and next-raise dependency, imitation risk
once the model is proven, and sequencing — which market must be won first for
the rest to be reachable. Rule 3 still applies, just in the future tense: the
question is whether a credible path to returns above cost of capital exists,
and what must be true along it.

## Mature companies

The center of gravity moves to `management-and-capital-allocation.md` and
`corporate-strategy.md`: saturation, productivity, portfolio pruning, cash
deployment, cannibalization discipline, organizational inertia, and renewal —
is the company optimizing the existing business at the expense of the next
one? Harvesting is a legitimate strategy *when chosen deliberately and priced
honestly*; the failure mode is harvesting by drift while narrating
transformation (the financial-performance module's decomposition catches
this).

## Technology and platform companies

Test network effects instead of asserting them: which side values which side,
where multi-homing breaks the lock, whether data actually improves the
product or just accumulates. Winner-take-most dynamics make position more
valuable and more binary — the industry-structure module should say which
regime this market is in. Marginal economics (near-zero cost of the next
unit) sharpen both scale advantages and price wars. Watch ecosystem
governance (developer/partner trust as an asset), infrastructure
dependencies, and technology-cycle exposure — including whether AI shifts the
cost curve the advantage rests on. Technology is not an advantage; a
technology-enabled mechanism with an imitation barrier is.

## Regulated industries (banking, healthcare, energy, telecom, pharma, utilities, defense, aviation)

Regulation is industry structure, not merely a risk line: it sets entry
barriers, price floors and ceilings, capital requirements, and sometimes the
customer itself (reimbursement, procurement). Analyze the regulator as an
actor with interests — direction of regulatory travel, the company's standing
with it, exposure concentration in one rule or one government customer, and
compliance cost as a scale advantage (a fixed cost incumbents amortize and
entrants cannot). A moat granted by regulation is real but rented: its
durability is political, and the falsification table should carry a
regulatory row.
