# competitive-advantage — where the advantage comes from, and whether it survives

## Question this answers

Where does this company's advantage actually come from, and will it still
exist in three to five years?

## When to run / when to skip

Run in every tier and standalone for any moat, durability, or "why does it
win" question. Skip only when the question is purely about industry
attractiveness with no company position at stake.

## Inputs

- From `competitive-position.md`: the next-best alternative and the delta a
  customer perceives — an advantage is an explanation of that delta.
- From `industry-structure.md`: the attribution verdict — how much of the
  return is positional and therefore needs explaining at all.
- From `business-model.md`: what drives margin and ROIC — the advantage must
  show up in one of those drivers or it is decoration.
- Standalone: reconstruct those three in a paragraph each before starting.

## Method

1. **List candidates, then cut hard.** One canonical list — scale, scope,
   learning effects, network effects, switching costs, brand, IP, proprietary
   data or technology, distribution, location, scarce inputs, regulation,
   customer relationships, ecosystem control, organizational routines. Beside
   it, the impostors: favorable market conditions, first-mover timing,
   competitor underinvestment, an industry tailwind, good execution. Most
   companies have one or two real advantages; a list of six is a list of
   unexamined candidates.
2. **Build the causal chain for each survivor** — every link, in order: what
   creates it → why customers care → the economic consequence (willingness to
   pay, acquisition cost, retention, pricing power, unit cost) → why imitation
   is slow or expensive *for a rival with money* → what organizational system
   reinforces it → strengthening or eroding, and why. A chain missing the
   imitation link is a strength, not an advantage.
3. **Apply VRIO as a filter, not a table**: valuable, rare, costly to
   imitate, organized to exploit — and add the question VRIO omits: durable
   against the specific changes already identified in the environment and
   industry modules?
4. **Separate resources from capabilities.** Resources are what it has
   (assets, brand, data, cash); capabilities are what it reliably does
   (product development, integration of acquisitions, pricing, supply chain).
   Classify the capabilities that matter: threshold (needed to play),
   differentiating (win today), dynamic (able to build the next advantage).
   Name capability gaps — they constrain every option downstream.
5. **Verdict**, with mechanism: **durable** / **contested** / **temporary**,
   plus direction of travel and the single capability most worth protecting.

## What good looks like

> "Strong brand" resolves, on inspection, into something narrower: in a
> category where product failure is costly and quality is unobservable before
> purchase, the brand functions as insurance. That is why it commands a
> mid-teens price premium at retail while private label wins on shelf price
> [Fact: scanner-data premium, trade press]. It is rare, and imitation is
> slow because the trust was accumulated through decades of not failing. But
> the reinforcing system is weakening: review platforms now make quality
> observable before purchase, substituting for exactly the uncertainty the
> brand monetizes. Verdict: real, eroding at the low end first — contested,
> not durable [Inference].

## Hands off

- The **verdict and direction** → `synthesis/diagnosis.md` — usually the
  spine of the central tension.
- **Which capabilities transfer** to adjacent markets, and which are anchored
  here → `growth-and-ma.md`.
- **Capability gaps** → `synthesis/alternatives.md` as feasibility
  constraints.
- **Erosion vectors** (what kills the advantage fastest) →
  `scenarios-and-risk.md`.

## Failure modes

- Naming a resource and stopping — "the data", "the brand" — with no economic
  consequence attached.
- Durability argued from history: twenty profitable years prove the past, not
  the mechanism.
- The impostor list presented as moats — a tailwind is not an advantage.
- Six advantages, none with a complete causal chain — always fewer, deeper.
- VRIO filled in as a ritual table with yes/no cells and no imitation
  economics.
- Skipping the organized link: an advantage nobody's incentives protect
  erodes by default.
