# competitive-position — how it competes, against whom, and why customers choose it

## Question this answers

Against the strategically relevant competitor set, why does a rational
customer choose this company instead of the next-best alternative?

## When to run / when to skip

Run in standard and full analyses, and standalone for "compare X and Y",
positioning, or "who is the real competition" questions. Skip only when the
advantage module is being run on an already-established position.

## Inputs

- The willingness-to-pay mechanism from `business-model.md` — position is that
  mechanism tested against alternatives.
- Industry forces from `industry-structure.md` where they shape who competes.
- Fact-pack peer table when relative margins, returns, or intensity ratios
  are cited.

## Method

1. **Choose the competitor set by relevance, not size.** Direct rivals,
   yes — but also the substitute provider taking the job a different way, the
   emerging entrant whose cost structure ignores the industry's assumptions,
   and the adjacent giant one decision away from entering. Three to six names,
   each with one line on why it is in the set. The most dangerous competitor
   is often not the largest.
2. **Read the strategy off the choices, not the slides.** Cost leadership,
   differentiation, focus, platform/ecosystem — inferred from what the company
   actually does: its relative price, cost structure, segment focus, product
   breadth, distribution, and what it visibly refuses to do. A claimed
   differentiation strategy with no price premium and no cost penalty is a
   company stuck in the middle.
3. **Compare only on dimensions that decide purchases.** A relative table —
   this company vs the set — on the four to six attributes customers actually
   trade off (price, performance, switching cost, availability, service,
   trust). Everything else is trivia. Use better/par/worse with a mechanism
   note, not scores without reasons.
4. **Answer the rational-customer question explicitly**, per major segment if
   answers differ: who is the next-best alternative, what is the delta, and is
   that delta priced? A position you cannot state as this sentence is not yet
   understood.
5. **Direction.** Is the gap to the next-best alternative widening or
   closing, and because of whose actions?

## What good looks like

> The relevant set is not the two listed rivals but the in-house alternative:
> the largest customers can self-supply at scale, and every pricing
> negotiation happens in that shadow. Customers below self-supply scale choose
> the company because integration cost, not product quality, is the decisive
> attribute — it is the only vendor pre-certified across all three legacy
> platforms. That delta is real but narrowing: the certification moat matters
> less each year the legacy platforms shrink [Inference; platform mix from
> filings].

## Hands off

- The **next-best alternative and the delta** → `competitive-advantage.md`,
  which must explain why the delta survives imitation.
- Relative price/cost position → `financial-performance.md` (do the margins
  agree?).
- The competitors with means and motive to retaliate →
  `synthesis/alternatives.md` for the response test.

## Failure modes

- The competitor set as a market-share leaderboard — size is not relevance.
- Taking the company's stated strategy at face value instead of reading its
  choices.
- Comparison tables on twelve dimensions where two decide purchases.
- A position described without naming the next-best alternative — against
  nothing, everything looks differentiated.
- Ignoring asymmetry: competitors with different economics respond to
  different incentives.
