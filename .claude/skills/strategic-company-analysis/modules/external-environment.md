# external-environment — the outside forces that can change the economics

## Question this answers

Which political, economic, social, technological, environmental, or legal
forces can materially change this company's strategic position — and through
what mechanism?

## When to run / when to skip

Run in a full analysis, and standalone when asked about macro exposure,
regulation, or disruption. Skip when the question is purely internal
(capabilities, management, business model mechanics) — or when the honest
answer is that no external force is currently first-order, in which case say
that in two sentences and move on.

## Inputs

- The business model's profit engine — a force matters only if it reaches it.
- Standalone: a one-paragraph sketch of how the company makes money.

## Method

1. **Scan, do not fill in.** Walk PESTEL as a checklist — policy and
   geopolitics; rates, cycles, currency, input costs; demographics and
   behavior shifts; technology and new business models; climate and resource
   constraints; regulation and antitrust. The scan is a sieve, not a
   deliverable: a complete six-box PESTEL is explicitly not wanted.
2. **Keep only forces with a mechanism.** For each force that survives: the
   mechanism into this company's economics (demand, price, cost, capital,
   permission to operate), direction and rough timing, who in the industry is
   advantaged or hurt, and what the company can actually do about it.
   Typically two to four forces survive; if eight did, the sieve failed.
3. **Separate level from change.** A permanent condition everyone faces
   (e.g. the industry is cyclical) shapes the industry chapter; what belongs
   here is *change* — a force that is strengthening, weakening, or newly
   arrived.
4. **Flag the uncertainties.** Forces whose direction is genuinely unknowable
   are not resolved here — they are named and handed to scenarios.

## What good looks like

> Of the six PESTEL boxes, one matters at first order: regulation. The
> pending data-portability rules would let customers export exactly the usage
> history that makes switching costly, converting the company's main retention
> mechanism into a compliance artifact. Timing is 18–36 months, the mechanism
> is direct (churn ↑ → lifetime value ↓ → the CAC math that justifies current
> spend breaks), and unlike its smaller rivals the company cannot arbitrage
> jurisdictions [Fact: draft regulation published; Inference: churn effect].
> Rates, demographics, and energy costs move results but not position — noted
> and set aside.

## Hands off

- Surviving forces that alter entry barriers, substitutes, or supplier/buyer
  power → `industry-structure.md`.
- Forces that attack a specific advantage mechanism →
  `competitive-advantage.md` (erosion vectors).
- Unresolvable uncertainties → `scenarios-and-risk.md` as scenario axes.
- Anything requiring a strategic response now → `synthesis/diagnosis.md`.

## Failure modes

- The six-box PESTEL dump — every cell filled, nothing diagnosed.
- Forces without mechanisms ("AI is a threat") — name the path to the P&L or
  drop it.
- Treating a permanent industry condition as news.
- Ignoring timing: a force ten years out and one two quarters out cannot carry
  the same weight.
- Silently resolving a genuine uncertainty instead of handing it to scenarios.
