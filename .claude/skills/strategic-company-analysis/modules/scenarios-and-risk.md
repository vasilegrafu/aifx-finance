# scenarios-and-risk — where the strategy breaks, and what would be seen first

## Question this answers

Under which plausible future conditions does this company's position or the
recommended strategy break — and what observable signal would fire first?

## When to run / when to skip

Run in every full analysis, after the analytical modules and before synthesis
completes — it consumes their uncertainties. Standalone for risk-assessment
and "what could go wrong" questions. Skip in a quick take, where "what to
watch" covers the need in three lines.

## Inputs

- Unresolvable uncertainties from `external-environment.md` (scenario axes).
- Strengthening forces from `industry-structure.md`.
- Advantage **erosion vectors** from `competitive-advantage.md`.
- Bottlenecks and dependencies from `value-chain.md`, misalignments from
  `management-and-capital-allocation.md`, leverage from
  `financial-performance.md`.

## Method

1. **Build three scenarios from the handed-in uncertainties** — base, upside,
   downside — around the one or two axes that actually decide outcomes, not
   generic optimism/pessimism. Each scenario: the external conditions, what
   they do to this company's economics (mechanism, rough magnitude), the
   plausible company response, and the **early-warning indicator** — the
   observable, checkable signal that this scenario is arriving, with a rough
   threshold.
2. **Prioritize risks; never catalogue.** From the handed-in risk candidates
   plus market, technology, regulatory, financial, operational, execution,
   and reputation checks: keep the few that combine real probability with
   real impact. For each: the mechanism (how it destroys value here,
   specifically), probability in words, magnitude via rule 6 arithmetic,
   mitigation actually available, and its leading indicator.
3. **Find the robust moves.** Which strategic actions make sense across all
   three scenarios, and which are bets on one? The distinction prices
   flexibility: an option that survives the downside is worth more than its
   base-case arithmetic shows.
4. **Check risk interactions.** The dangerous cases are correlated —
   leverage plus cyclical demand plus a covenant is one risk, not three.

## What good looks like

> The axis that decides is not demand — all three scenarios keep it within a
> band the balance sheet tolerates — but the substitute's cost curve. In the
> downside, its unit cost crosses parity around year three [Assumption:
> current learning rate persists], at which point the company's price umbrella
> becomes the substitute's adoption subsidy. Early warning: substitute's
> quoted prices in public tenders, checkable quarterly, threshold parity
> minus 10%. The robust move across all three scenarios is the certification
> -led expansion; the bet-on-one-scenario move is the capacity doubling,
> which only pays in the upside.

## Hands off

- **Early-warning indicators with thresholds** →
  `synthesis/falsification.md` — they become the monitoring half of the KPI
  set.
- **Robust vs bet moves** → `synthesis/recommendation.md`.
- Downside mechanisms and magnitudes → `synthesis/diagnosis.md` sizing and
  `synthesis/alternatives.md` option risk.

## Failure modes

- Scenarios as ±20% on the base case — a spread is not a scenario; the axis
  must be a named uncertainty with a mechanism.
- The exhaustive risk register — thirty risks, none sized, none monitored.
- Risks without leading indicators: an unwatchable risk is a worry, not an
  analysis.
- Treating correlated exposures as independent line items.
- Scenario work that never feeds back into which strategy to prefer.
