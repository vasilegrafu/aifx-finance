# diagnosis — stop collecting facts; decide what actually matters

## Question this answers

Which three to five strategic issues actually matter, what causes each, and
what is the central tension management must resolve?

## When to run / when to skip

Run when the analytical modules are done — this is where collection stops.
Standalone only when the user brings their own facts and asks "so what is the
real problem?". Never skip in standard or full tier: modules without a
diagnosis are the disconnected tables this skill exists to prevent.

## Inputs

- Every **Hands off** block from the modules that ran. They are the raw
  material; this file adds no new research.
- The central strategic question from the hub's *Before any module* step.

## Method

1. **Force the cut to 3–5 issues.** Everything the modules surfaced competes
   for these slots. An issue earns one by economic magnitude and strategic
   consequence — not by how much analysis it took to find.
2. **Write each issue in full**: the issue (what is happening) → evidence
   (which module findings, with their Fact/Inference labels) → root cause
   (why it is happening) → strategic consequence (why it matters) →
   **magnitude** (rule 6: the order-of-magnitude arithmetic, shown) → time
   horizon → the decision it forces on management.
3. **Dig to root cause with a causal tree when the chain is long.** Slowing
   profitability → weak pricing power → commoditizing product → eroding
   differentiation: keep asking why until the answer is something management
   can influence, then mark which links they control. Symptoms make headlines;
   only root causes make issues.
4. **Hunt the contradiction.** Growth ambition vs capital discipline, premium
   position vs mass expansion, harvest-margins vs reinvest, centralize vs
   localize. The central tension is usually a contradiction between two
   things the company is visibly trying to do at once — name it as the answer
   to the hub's central question.
5. **SWOT is permitted here and only here**, as a half-page synthesis where
   every cell cites a module finding and carries its mechanism. If a cell
   could appear in another company's SWOT unchanged, it is not finished.

## What good looks like

> **Issue 2 of 4 — the margin story is a harvesting story.** Evidence:
> operating margin +4pts over five years while R&D intensity fell 14%→10%
> against peers' stable 13–15% [Fact: fact pack]; advantage verdict
> "contested, eroding at the low end" [Inference, advantage module]. Root
> cause: an incentive plan paying on adjusted EPS makes harvesting the
> rational behavior [management module]. Consequence: current returns are
> partly borrowed from the future position. Magnitude: restoring peer-level
> intensity costs ~3pts of operating margin ≈ $600M/yr against ~$18B revenue
> [arithmetic shown]. Horizon: the spend gap compounds; 2–3 years before the
> product gap is visible. Decision forced: re-invest and reset market
> expectations, or harvest deliberately and return the cash — the current
> middle path does neither.

## Hands off

- The prioritized issues, each with its forced decision →
  `synthesis/alternatives.md` — options exist to resolve issues, nothing
  else.
- The central tension → `synthesis/recommendation.md` — the recommendation
  must resolve it explicitly.
- Evidence labels carried on each issue → `synthesis/falsification.md`.

## Failure modes

- Twelve "key issues" — a priority list longer than five is a table of
  contents.
- Symptoms promoted to issues because they are visible (the share price is
  never an issue; it is a symptom of one).
- Issues without magnitude arithmetic, so priority is rhetorical.
- A diagnosis that is a summary — restating the modules instead of deciding
  between them.
- Generic SWOT smuggled in — any cell portable to another company fails.
- Burying the contradiction because it is uncomfortable; it is usually the
  finding.
