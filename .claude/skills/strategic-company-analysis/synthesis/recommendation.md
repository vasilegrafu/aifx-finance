# recommendation — one direction, defended, staged, and measurable

## Question this answers

What should management do over the next three to five years, why is that
better than the alternatives, and how will anyone know it is working?

## When to run / when to skip

Run after `synthesis/alternatives.md`; standalone only if the user supplies
the options and asks for the call. Never skip when a recommendation was
requested — an analysis that trails off into "it depends" has not finished.

## Inputs

- Ranked options with post-retaliation economics from
  `synthesis/alternatives.md`.
- The central tension from `synthesis/diagnosis.md` — the recommendation must
  resolve it, not straddle it.
- Execution credibility from `management-and-capital-allocation.md`;
  robust-vs-bet classification from `scenarios-and-risk.md`.

## Method

1. **Choose one primary direction.** A portfolio of moves is legitimate only
   when the diagnosis genuinely demands parallel action — never as a hedge
   against choosing. State, in order: what management should do; why it wins
   against the named alternatives (their actual weaknesses, not strawmen);
   **what must be true** for it to work — the three load-bearing assumptions,
   stated as testable propositions; what to **stop doing** (a strategy that
   frees no resources funds itself with wishes); which capabilities must be
   built; which risks must be actively managed.
2. **Stage the implementation**: 0–12 months (immediate moves and the
   stop-doings), 12–24 (capability building, proof points), 24–60 (the
   position the strategy is buying). Few initiatives, each with owner where
   identifiable, capital, and milestone. Not a project plan — the test is
   whether each stage's failure would be *visible* at the next milestone.
3. **Pick the KPIs from the falsification loop, plus value.** The leading
   indicators are precisely the assumption-falsifiers and early warnings
   handed to `synthesis/falsification.md` — measurement and monitoring are
   one system, not two lists. Add the two or three lagging value measures
   (ROIC trend, FCF, the segment share that matters). Under eight in total.
4. **Score the position — anchored, or not at all.** Ten dimensions
   (industry attractiveness, competitive position, advantage durability,
   capabilities, financial health, growth potential, flexibility, execution
   capability, risk exposure, overall outlook), 1–10, **anchored**: 2 =
   structurally disadvantaged or broken; 5 = par with the median named peer;
   8 = clearly superior with an identified mechanism likely to hold 3–5
   years. Score against the named peer set, and explain any score ≤3 or ≥8.
   Unanchored scores are decoration and are worse than none.
5. **Close with the final judgment**: one short paragraph answering *what
   must this company do to create and sustain superior economic value over
   the next 3–5 years* — the sentence an executive repeats in the elevator.

## What good looks like

> Recommendation: cede the low end and deepen the certified segment (Option
> C). B is inferior because its economics invert post-retaliation; A
> (status quo plus buybacks) is inferior because it finances the insurgent's
> ladder up-market. Must be true: (1) certification stays a 3-year barrier
> [regulatory assumption]; (2) the segment's switching costs hold as
> contracts renew [testable at each renewal cycle]; (3) freed low-end capacity
> is actually shut, not repurposed to chase volume [a stop-doing with an
> owner]. If (1) falls, this strategy has no moat and reverts to B's
> problem — which is why regulatory drafts are KPI #1, not a footnote.

## Hands off

- The three **what-must-be-true assumptions**, verbatim →
  `synthesis/falsification.md` — they are its raw material.
- Staging and KPIs → the final document per `output-forms.md`.

## Failure modes

- The unchosen choice: a "balanced portfolio of initiatives" that resolves no
  tension.
- Alternatives dismissed as strawmen rather than defeated on their merits.
- No stop-doing — additive strategy with invented capacity.
- "What must be true" missing, vague, or untestable.
- KPIs invented separately from the falsifiers, so monitoring and strategy
  drift apart.
- Unanchored 1–10 scores; or a 9 with no mechanism and no explanation.
- A recommendation this management's incentives will strangle, delivered
  without that flag.
